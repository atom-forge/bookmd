# 08.04. Figma – Presenting your project

## Objective

Create a short, easy-to-follow UX presentation of your project using Figma or FigJam. A good presentation makes clear whose problem you are solving, which decisions you made, and what evidence supports them; it is more than a collection of screens.

## Connection to the course

> [!note] Key idea
> Build a story in five or six frames: the problem and participant, the task or research question, the solution's key screen, the test task, an important finding, and finally the next iteration. Present test results through specific observations rather than general satisfaction claims.

## A presentation as a chain of evidence

A UX case study is easy to follow when it shows the reasons behind decisions rather than opening with the final screen. **Narrative** here means the logic of the project story: who the user is, what their problem is, what evidence you collected, what the solution rests on, and what testing taught you. Screens are persuasive only when viewers understand which problem they address.

A **case study** presents a project through decisions and evidence, rather than simply collecting portfolio images. **Design rationale** is a brief explanation of why you chose a particular layout, label, or interaction. End by identifying limitations and the next iteration too. This makes the presentation more credible: it shows that design is a learning process.

## Terms

**Narrative:** the deliberate, understandable order of project information.  
**Case study:** a project presentation connecting the problem, process, decisions, and results.  
**Design rationale:** a brief, evidence-linked justification of a design decision.  
**Iteration:** a cycle of design improvement based on observations or feedback.
