# Figma – FigJam

## Objective

In this session, organize your project's initial ideas on a FigJam board. FigJam is the Figma ecosystem's shared visual workspace: it supports brainstorming, mind mapping, and drawing simple user flows before interface design begins.

## Connection to the course

> [!note] Key idea
> Create a short board about your chosen problem: identify the user, their goal, the obstacle, and the sign of success, then connect them. The board should help reveal what research still needs to clarify, rather than collect finished solutions.

## How should you think in a FigJam board?

A **board** is an infinite canvas that can be edited collaboratively. What matters is that it creates visible connections between scattered ideas. Use separate areas for the problem, user, assumptions, and open questions. Put one statement on each sticky note so that you can rearrange, group, or discard them easily later.

A **mind map** is a branching structure extending from a central concept. It is useful when you are still exploring which topics to investigate. A **user flow**, by contrast, shows a sequence over time: the steps users take to achieve their goal. In a flow, show decisions and important state changes rather than graphical screen details, for example: “search for an appointment → filter → select an appointment → confirmation.”

## Terms

**Board:** a shared visual workspace for organizing notes, diagrams, and connections.  
**Sticky note:** a brief, independently movable idea or observation on a board.  
**Mind map:** a branching diagram exploring a topic's concepts and connections.  
**User flow:** the sequence of user steps and decisions toward a goal.
