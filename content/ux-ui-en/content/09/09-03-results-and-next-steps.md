# Results and next steps

## Objectives

Students will be able to compare results against predefined success criteria, match their claims to the strength of the evidence, and identify a next step that reduces real uncertainty at the end of the project.

## What counts as a result?

> [!note] Key idea
> A result is more than a new prototype being finished. The question is what changed in the user task. Return to the earlier success criterion: did more participants complete the task? Were there fewer critical misunderstandings? Did they correctly interpret the consequences? Write observations and conclusions separately.

For example: “After selection, the three participants in the second round correctly stated that the booking was not yet final.” This is an observation. “The revised status and button label probably reduced misunderstanding in the situation studied.” This is a conclusion. Do not claim the problem is solved for every user after only a small prototype test.

## Results together with limitations

A credible report does not hide limitations. Participants may already have known the domain; the prototype may not have included real payment; testing may have taken place in controlled conditions; or you may have examined only one device size. These do not invalidate learning; they clarify what it applies to.

Also describe what did not change or became worse. A new explanation may improve first use while slowing routine tasks. The aim is to provide an honest basis for the next decision, rather than a flawless story.

## Choosing the next step

The next step should connect to a specific question rather than simply be “further development.” If content comprehension is uncertain, further research or content testing is needed. If the prototype works but technical constraints are unknown, investigate feasibility. If a high-risk exceptional state remains untested, address it in the next round.

When prioritizing, consider user impact, uncertainty, and the cost of change. Not every idea belongs in the next step. An open question matters when a wrong answer would substantially change the solution or service risk.

## Worked example

In a prototype's second test round, the main booking task succeeds, but participants interpret failed payment differently. The result is therefore that selection and finalization have become clearer while recovery from payment failure remains uncertain, rather than “the flow is finished.” The next step is to prototype the error state and test it in a new task, rather than add features.

## Review questions

1. Which success criterion has direct observational evidence?
2. What limitation must you state alongside the result?
3. What is your biggest open question, and why does it matter?
4. What would be the next proportionate research or testing step?

## Glossary

**Result:** a change or lesson observed in a study relative to the success criterion.  
**Conclusion:** an interpretation drawn from observations with confidence proportionate to uncertainty.  
**Limitation:** a condition bounding the validity or interpretation of a result.  
**Next step:** a research, design, or implementation task chosen to reduce specific uncertainty.
