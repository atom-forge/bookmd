# Figma – User testing and feedback

## Objective

Learn to turn a Figma prototype into testable research material. During testing, give realistic tasks, observe decisions, and record signs of uncertainty, errors, or detours rather than asking whether participants like the interface.

## Connection to the course

> [!note] Key idea
> Write a short scenario for your prototype: who the participant is, what they want to achieve, where they start, and what indicates success. Record where they pause, what they say aloud, and what they misunderstand. Document changes resulting from feedback as responses to observations rather than taste.

## A test is not a demonstration

A **usability test** observes whether a representative participant can independently achieve a goal. The facilitator gives a task, such as “Find an appointment tomorrow afternoon and book it.” This differs from demonstrating the solution and asking whether participants like it. The latter collects opinions; a test examines actual behavior.

**Moderation** supports participants without solving the task for them. Ask neutral follow-ups: “What do you expect will happen?” or “What are you looking for now?” **Observation** records visible signs: incorrect clicks, long pauses, uncertain statements, or unexpected detours. A **finding** is usable when it describes the situation, user impact, and supporting observation.

## Terms

**Usability test:** an observational study of whether participants can complete a task.  
**Task scenario:** test instructions describing the participant's situation, goal, and starting point.  
**Moderation:** neutral facilitation of a test without influencing participants.  
**Finding:** a documented usability problem and its evidence.
