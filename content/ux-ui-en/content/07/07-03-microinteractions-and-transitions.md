# Microinteractions and transitions

## Objectives

Students will distinguish microinteractions that support a task from mere decoration. They will deliberately design what a transition communicates, how long it lasts, and how it remains manageable for people with motion sensitivity or limited attention.

## Small feedback, significant uncertainty

> [!note] Key idea
> A microinteraction is brief feedback tied to one goal: field validity, saving, selection, a successful action, or a changed quantity. Its value lies in whether users understand the system's state, rather than how impressive it looks. A brief “Saved” message accompanied by visibly updated data reduces repeated clicks and doubt.

A transition can help users follow a change: opening a card's details can indicate that a deeper view of the same element is appearing. But transitions that are too long, too frequent, or impossible to skip slow routine tasks and can be disruptive. Motion should never be the only way content is communicated.

## Timing and control

Good transitions are brief, predictable, and proportionate to the action. Avoid animations that prevent users from continuing or distract them after every small choice. For automatic motion, flashing, or continuously changing content, pausing and respecting reduced-motion settings are particularly important.

## Worked example

After appointment selection, a booking interface changes only the button's color. Users are unsure whether the choice has actually reached the summary. Better feedback updates the selected appointment's state, the summary, and the ability to continue together. Animation can help make the connection visible, but cannot replace a textual state indication.

## Review questions

1. Which microinteraction would reduce real uncertainty in your project?
2. Is there any motion without which users would not understand a change?
3. Which transition slows the task more than it helps?

## Glossary

**Microinteraction:** brief feedback tied to a single user action or state.  
**Transition:** a visual change between interface or system states.  
**Reduced motion:** a setting that reduces unnecessary animation for users.  
**Timing:** the duration and moment of appearance of feedback or a transition.
