# Figma – Prototyping and interactions

## Objective

Turn static wireframes into a navigable prototype. Its purpose is to make a specific user task and its feedback testable, rather than look like a developed product.

## Connection to the course

> [!note] Key idea
> Connect your earlier screens into a short task flow of two or three steps. Include a starting state, at least one decision point, and clear feedback. Observe whether hierarchy and button cues let users anticipate what happens after a click.

## What does a prototype reveal?

A **prototype** is a model of connected screens simulating a usage situation. It does not prove technical functionality; it tests whether users understand the interface and can work through the task. A **trigger** is the initiating event, such as pressing a button; an **interaction** describes the response, such as opening another screen or displaying a panel.

A **transition** is how a screen or state change appears. A restrained transition can convey that users remain within the same process, but cannot replace clear labels or visible feedback. **Fidelity** indicates the prototype's level of detail. Low-fi is often sufficient for early tests; increase detail when the question being investigated actually requires it.

## Terms

**Prototype:** a testable design that simulates interactions.  
**Trigger:** the event that initiates an interaction.  
**Transition:** the presentation of a change between states or screens.  
**Fidelity:** a design's level of detail and realism.
