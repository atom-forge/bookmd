# Portfolio case study and reflective conclusion

## Objectives

Students will be able to turn their course project into a portfolio case study showing the process, their role, evidence, and lessons learned. They will understand reflection as analysis supporting the next professional decision, rather than self-justification or self-criticism.

## What makes a good portfolio case study?

> [!note] Key idea
> A portfolio case study is not a screen gallery. It shows the problem you solved, the constraints you worked under, your responsibilities, the evidence you collected, and how learning changed the solution. Final visuals matter, but the quality of the work can be understood only through the decision-making process.

Be precise about your role. Even individual projects can involve external rules, sources, or participants; in group work, it is especially important not to take credit for someone else's decisions. Describe time, technical, and research constraints too, because they provide the real context for decisions.

## Reflecting through evidence

Good reflection is specific: “In research, I asked about interface solutions too early, so the initial insights were weak. In later interviews, I asked about past examples, which helped me understand the decision situation better.” This matters because it shows how the working method changed, rather than merely admitting a mistake.

Do not write “next time I would do everything better.” Choose one or two high-impact lessons and name the next practical step. This might be earlier testing, more targeted participant recruitment, accessibility checks, or earlier discussion of a technical constraint.

## Suggested case study structure

1. Context, role, and project constraints.  
2. User problem and goal.  
3. Research and insights.  
4. Design process, key decisions, and prototype.  
5. Testing, iteration, and results.  
6. Lessons, open questions, and next steps.

In each section, give one or two strong, verifiable examples rather than general claims. Anonymize participants and do not publish sensitive information or screenshots without permission.

## Worked example

A student designed a booking process. The first version focused on the calendar, but research revealed that cancellation conditions and confirmation caused greater uncertainty. The portfolio case study therefore shows the insight, the changed summary state, test observations, and the need for further investigation of payment failure handling, rather than simply claiming to have “redesigned the calendar.” This is more credible than presenting a supposedly flawless final product.

## Review questions

1. What can a reader learn about you beyond the final screens?
2. Which constraint influenced your most important decision?
3. What specific lesson would change the first week of your next project?
4. What data or images must you anonymize?

## Glossary

**Portfolio case study:** a professional account of a project's problem, process, decisions, and results.  
**Reflection:** a specific, evidence-linked interpretation of decisions and learning.  
**Own role:** clearly identified responsibility and contribution within the project.  
**Open question:** uncertainty the project has not yet resolved.
